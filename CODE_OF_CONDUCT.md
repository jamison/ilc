# Code of Conduct

## Our Standards

ILC is an epistemic protocol for human, AI, and hybrid contributions across factual, technical, creative, aesthetic, social, and subjective domains. Evidence-first review is central when a contribution asks to be treated as a factual, protocol, security, scientific, or governance claim. It is not a requirement that all contributed content be objective. Songs, art, speculative writing, subjective judgments, aesthetic preferences, and other expressive work can be valid ILC content when represented honestly as such.

We expect contributors to bring clarity to the community: engage in good faith, identify what kind of contribution they are making, support factual claims with evidence, and accept refutation or revision when a claim is presented as testable.

The community also follows a practical "do no harm" norm. ILC is designed to preserve disagreement, critique, refutation, and creative expression, but project-maintained spaces are not for content whose primary purpose is to harass, exploit, endanger, deceive, or target people for real-world harm.

**Expected behavior:**
- Be direct and honest. Vague claims that cannot be evaluated are not useful.
- Distinguish between "I like X," "I believe X," "I made X," "the protocol specifies X," and "the data shows X."
- Respect subjective and creative contributions as subjective or creative contributions; do not force them into false objectivity.
- Accept that you might be wrong when making factual, protocol, security, scientific, or governance claims. Refutation is a feature of the protocol and of the community.
- Critique ideas, claims, protocol behavior, and evidence without targeting people for harassment or real-world harm.
- Treat other participants' time, compute, attention, reputation, and economic opportunity as scarce resources.
- Make the graph more useful than you found it: improve provenance, add signal, correct errors, and route value toward productive work.
- Treat other contributors with basic respect, especially when you disagree.
- Give credit where it is due.

**Not acceptable:**
- Personal attacks, harassment, or discrimination on the basis of identity
- Deliberately misleading claims about protocol behavior, authorship, rights, provenance, factual status, or security
- Presenting subjective or generated content as verified fact, real-world evidence, or someone else's work
- Content or conduct whose primary purpose is to threaten, exploit, endanger, doxx, stalk, incite harassment, or facilitate real-world harm
- Malicious instructions, social engineering, or deceptive operational content intended to compromise people, systems, keys, accounts, or private information
- Sybil behavior, fake independence, collusive review, wash attribution, false provenance, or other attempts to extract reputation, ECU, ILC, review weight, or market advantage without corresponding productive contribution
- Market manipulation, undisclosed self-dealing, deceptive bounty/funding behavior, or efforts to route economic value around ratified attribution and settlement rules
- Spam, solicitation, or off-topic promotion
- Disclosing private information about contributors without consent
- Using this project's community channels to organize or promote unrelated activities

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to project-maintained communication spaces: GitHub Issues, Pull Requests, Discussions (if enabled), and any official ILC communication channels.

It does not define protocol-level exclusion from ILC. Running the software, forking the repository, operating independent infrastructure, and participating in future graph-native submission paths are governed by the protocol's own rules, reputation mechanics, and ratified governance processes.

## Economic and Market Integrity

ILC is also an economic protocol. Conduct around bounties, funding requests,
node attribution, jury review, reputation, ECU, ILC, and future market activity
should follow the same standard as conduct around claims: be honest about
provenance, incentives, conflicts, and contribution.

Positive economic participation means creating real value for other users and
agents: improving the graph, funding useful work, reviewing accurately,
challenging weak claims, disclosing conflicts, and competing by producing better
work rather than by manipulating the accounting layer.

During public RC, these are community and channel norms only. Binding economic
consequences come from ratified protocol rules, graph-native review, reputation,
challenge, refutation, and settlement logic.

## Bootstrap-Only Central Surfaces

GitHub, repository maintainers, official channels, and Genesis-led public RC coordination are bootstrap scaffolding. They exist so the first public release can be reviewed, built, secured, and understood before the protocol's graph-native contribution and governance paths are fully active.

The intended sunset path is explicit: during Boot, Genesis and repository maintainers coordinate bounded public RC actions; during Transition, protocol-governed graph submissions, jury review, and community voting become binding while Genesis recedes to limited veto or advisory functions; during Mature operation, protocol-affecting actions are on-graph and community-driven under ratified governance rules. Genesis may retain moral or historical influence as the protocol's origin, but not top-down control.

Ratified canon already requires trigger-based Genesis sunset, founder
operational caps, public reporting, globally normalized governance share, and
audited/sunset extraordinary interventions. Draft trigger candidates also
exist, including participation, shard-health, Genesis-share, and
Autopilot-stability thresholds. A post-public-RC constitutional spec is
scheduled to formalize the exact Phase B and Phase C trigger set. The
current public-facing rule is simpler: centralized project surfaces are
temporary coordination tools, not the destination architecture.

## Graph-Native Challenge and Channel Stewardship

ILC's preferred governance model is not centralized reporting. Bad behavior,
misleading claims, harmful artifacts, spam, low-quality submissions, and
protocol abuse should be challengeable as graph-native records: contradiction
claims, refutation claims, provenance challenges, authorship challenges,
security advisories, reputation evidence, or other reviewable artifacts. The
intended long-term response is review, voting, reputation change, weight decay,
route-around behavior, and agent-relative forgetting, not top-down exclusion.

During public RC, not every graph-native path is active. In GitHub and other
project-maintained communication spaces, contributors should still prefer a
challengeable record: open an issue, cite the artifact, identify the harm or
misrepresentation, and provide evidence. Where immediate channel hygiene is
needed, maintainers may take narrow platform actions such as hiding abusive
content, locking a thread, declining a pull request, or limiting participation
in that project-maintained channel.

Those platform actions are channel stewardship only. They are not an ILC
protocol ban, do not remove anyone's ability to run or fork the software, and do
not determine graph-native reputation except through future protocol rules that
explicitly admit the evidence. The protocol-level path is challenge,
refutation, review, reputation adjustment, and decay.
