## SIM-CORR-02 — Signal Strength × Correlation Phase Surface

**Research question:** At what (signal_strength, rho) does FCR cross an operationally significant threshold?

### Results

FCR surface (rows = signal level, columns = rho):

| Signal | value | ρ=0.0 | ρ=0.2 | ρ=0.4 | ρ=0.6 | ρ=0.8 | ρ=0.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| strong   | 1.0 | 0.0126 | 0.0167 | 0.0268 | 0.0243 | 0.0447 | 0.0374 |
| medium   | 0.5 | **0.1373** | **0.1573** | **0.1615** | **0.1736** | **0.1691** | **0.1835** |
| weak     | 0.2 | **0.3211** | **0.3275** | **0.3425** | **0.3525** | **0.3568** | **0.3483** |
| marginal | 0.1 | **0.4101** | **0.4145** | **0.4204** | **0.3986** | **0.4177** | **0.4190** |

Bold = FCR > 10% (operationally significant threshold).

### Prediction verdicts

**P1 CONFIRMED:** weak(0.2) + rho≥0.6 → FCR = 35-36% (threshold was >15%). ✓
**P2 CONFIRMED:** marginal(0.1) + rho≥0.4 → FCR = 40-42% (threshold was >30%). ✓
**P3 REFUTED:** No sharp phase transition observed. The transition between "safe" and "dangerous" is in signal space (strong→medium), not in rho space. ✗
**P4 PARTIAL:** Dangerous region (FCR>10%): medium@rho=any, weak@rho=any, marginal@rho=any — but **strong signal never exceeds 5% FCR at any rho**. Signal strength is the primary axis; rho is secondary.

### Key findings

**Finding 1: The phase transition is in signal space, not rho space.**
Rho matters — but it's the secondary axis. The dominant predictor of FCR is signal strength:

| Signal | FCR range across rho |
|---|---|
| strong   | 1.3% – 4.5% |
| medium   | **13.7% – 18.4%** |
| weak     | **32.1% – 35.7%** |
| marginal | **39.9% – 42.0%** |

The jump from strong (1.0) to medium (0.5) increases minimum FCR by **10.4× (1.3% → 13.7%)**. This is the dominant effect. Within each signal level, rho adds only 1-4 percentage points of additional FCR.

**Finding 2: P3 refuted — no sharp nonlinear rho threshold.**
The FCR surface within each signal level is flat across rho (variance ~1-2pp). There is no "critical rho" where FCR suddenly jumps. Rho degrades FCR smoothly and modestly once signal is weak.

**Finding 3: The protocol's real vulnerability axis is claim clarity, not validator correlation.**
Strong-signal claims (clear empirical questions with high expert consensus) remain safe at any rho. Weak-signal claims (genuine expert disagreement, ambiguous technical territory) have ~32% FCR regardless of validator correlation. This reframes the architectural priority: the protocol should route weak-signal claims through higher scrutiny, not attempt to enforce rho constraints.

**Finding 4: Rho matters more at medium signal than at weak/marginal.**
At medium signal, FCR increases from 13.7% (rho=0) to 18.4% (rho=0.9) — a 34% relative increase. At weak signal, the corresponding range is 32.1% → 35.7% — only 11% relative increase. At marginal, 41% → 42%. This is because at very weak signal, even independent honest voters are voting near-randomly, so correlation adds little additional damage.

**Finding 5: Revised architectural implication — claim routing by confidence interval.**
The protocol needs a mechanism to estimate signal strength (claim clarity) per domain at evaluation time. Claims with high inter-validator disagreement on test submissions should be routed to larger panels, sequential deliberation, or mandatory adversarial review — regardless of rho.

### Prediction verdicts

**P1 REFUTED:** weak+rho=0.6+alpha=20% → FCR=29.96% (threshold was >30%). Narrowly refuted.

### Key findings

**Finding 1: The effects are ADDITIVE, not superlinear.**
The superlinearity check shows joint/additive ratios consistently at 0.97–1.12× across all tested conditions. The rho×alpha(x) interaction term is negligible.

```
  medium, rho=0.6, alpha=20%: joint=22.0% additive=19.7% ratio=1.12×
  medium, rho=0.8, alpha=20%: joint=20.9% additive=20.6% ratio=1.01×
  weak,   rho=0.6, alpha=20%: joint=30.0% additive=28.8% ratio=1.04×
  weak,   rho=0.8, alpha=20%: joint=30.3% additive=30.4% ratio=1.00×
```

This is the most important finding from SIM-RATCHET-01. The epistemic ratchet mechanism does NOT produce superlinear compounding at the jury decision stage. Effects are essentially additive.

**The reviewer's hypothesis about the compounding chain was structurally correct but the compounding happens downstream (in the graph propagation stage), not at the jury vote level.** SIM-LAUNDER-01 is the correct experiment for the dangerous part.

**Finding 2: Rho is nearly orthogonal to cartel effect in this parameter range.**
For any fixed (signal, alpha), FCR varies by at most 1-3pp across rho values. Rho and alpha_x are effectively independent influences on FCR. They don't interact meaningfully at the jury decision stage.

**Finding 3: Signal strength is overwhelmingly the dominant variable — confirmed.**
SIM-CORR-01 showed strong signal stays safe. SIM-RATCHET-01 confirms: at strong signal, even 30% cartel control only reaches 16-17% FCR. At weak signal, 0% cartel already produces 16-19% FCR. Signal strength determines which regime you're in; cartel and rho shift you within it.

**Finding 4: The baseline FCR at weak signal (no cartel, honest validators) is the real problem.**
At weak signal + alpha=0 + rho=0 (fully independent honest experts): FCR = **15.9%**. This is intrinsic jury uncertainty on ambiguous claims, not an attack. The jury is correct only 48.6% of the time overall at weak signal — slightly worse than random, because the honest experts are genuinely confused. The adversary doesn't need to capture anything on marginal claims; the honest protocol already has a 16%+ false ratification rate.

**Finding 5: SIM-P2 shows medium signal with small cartel is not safe.**
Medium signal + alpha=10% + rho=0 → FCR=14.4%. P2 predicted FCR<10% for this regime. The protocol is not within acceptable FCR bounds even at medium signal with a 10% domain cartel. This is a genuine finding.

**Finding 6: Where the protocol is genuinely safe.**
Strong signal + alpha≤20%: FCR < 11%. This is the only clean safety region. The protocol's epistemic guarantees hold specifically for high-confidence, high-signal-strength claims where genuine expert consensus exists. Everything below "strong signal" requires additional mechanisms (larger panels, sequential deliberation, mandatory adversarial review, or claim routing).

## Cross-SIM synthesis — Phase 1 complete (4 SIMs run)

### The emergent model after 1.44M simulated claims

The two-session simulation program has produced a substantially different threat model than the initial hypotheses suggested.

**Original mental model (pre-simulation):**
```
correlation ↑
    → effective_n ↓
    → jury accuracy ↓↓↓
    → protocol breaks
```

**Revised model (post-simulation, four SIMs):**
```
Signal strength → regime (dominant axis)
  Strong signal: 95-98% accuracy at any rho, any alpha≤20%
  Medium signal: 87-86% accuracy, FCR=10-18% baseline
  Weak signal:   49-57% accuracy, FCR=16-44% regardless of rho
  Marginal:      44-52% accuracy, FCR=22-48% regardless of rho

Within each regime:
  Cartel effect:  roughly proportional to alpha_x (additive)
  Rho effect:     small additional damage (1-4pp FCR), no nonlinear threshold
  Joint effect:   additive (ratio ~1.0-1.1×), no superlinear compounding

Where attacks are dangerous:
  NOT: jury capture via majority (requires alpha>50%)
  YES: influence amplification on medium/weak-signal claims
  YES: baseline jury error on genuinely ambiguous claims (no attack needed)
  YES: downstream graph propagation of individual false ratifications
```

### Architectural implications — what actually needs to change

**1. Claim classification by signal strength is the top priority.**
Not rho detection. Not CDL-V3 modifications. The protocol needs:
- A mechanism to estimate signal strength (inter-validator disagreement as a proxy) at evaluation time
- Routing: strong-signal claims → standard 7-person panel; weak/marginal → mandatory larger panel or sequential deliberation
- Priority weighting by `confidence_deficit × influence × downstream_reuse` (the FCR × claim influence product)

**2. The epistemic ratchet is a GRAPH problem, not a JURY problem.**
SIM-RATCHET-01 proves the jury decision stage does not compound adversarial effects superlinearly. The dangerous compounding the reviewer predicted is correct — but it happens in the graph propagation stage, not at the vote. SIM-LAUNDER-01 is now clearly the highest-priority next experiment.

**3. Medium-signal claims require a separate design target.**
At medium signal + alpha=10%: FCR=14.4%. This is worse than the 10% threshold with only 10% domain expertise cartel. This cannot be addressed by jury size alone — the fundamental issue is that medium-signal claims have genuine inter-expert disagreement. Sequential deliberation (adversarial review after initial jury) or mandatory dissent documentation may be the correct mechanism.

**4. Werner decay should target medium-signal-domain cartels specifically.**
Sustained incorrect voting on medium-signal claims produces ECU decay — but only if correct validators accumulate refutation edges. The SIM-REPUTATION-01 experiment should specifically test whether Werner phase boundary (δ > R) holds under the medium-signal + 10% cartel condition found here.
