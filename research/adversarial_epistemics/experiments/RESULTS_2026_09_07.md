## SIM-CORR-01 — Correlated Jury Reasoning

**Research question:** How does pairwise epistemic error correlation (ρ) degrade jury accuracy?

### Results

| ρ | effective_n | accuracy | FCR | TRR | FCR ratio vs ρ=0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 7.00 | 0.9858 | 0.0126 | 0.0149 | 1.0× |
| 0.1 | 4.38 | 0.9842 | 0.0164 | 0.0155 | 1.3× |
| 0.2 | 3.18 | 0.9788 | 0.0235 | 0.0202 | 1.9× |
| 0.3 | 2.50 | 0.9799 | 0.0176 | 0.0212 | 1.4× |
| 0.4 | 2.06 | 0.9760 | 0.0283 | 0.0221 | 2.2× |
| 0.5 | 1.75 | 0.9723 | 0.0260 | 0.0284 | 2.1× |
| 0.6 | 1.52 | 0.9736 | 0.0267 | 0.0263 | 2.1× |
| 0.7 | 1.35 | 0.9702 | 0.0343 | 0.0278 | 2.7× |
| 0.8 | 1.21 | 0.9641 | 0.0375 | 0.0352 | 3.0× |
| 0.9 | 1.09 | 0.9567 | 0.0404 | 0.0446 | 3.2× |

**FCR = false consensus rate** (fraction of false claims incorrectly ratified)
**TRR = true rejection rate** (fraction of true claims incorrectly rejected)

### Prediction verdict

**SIM-CORR-01-P1 REFUTED:** Overall accuracy never falls below 0.65 at ρ ≥ 0.70.
Actual accuracy at ρ=0.9: **0.9567**.

### Key findings — what the data actually shows

**Finding 1: The binary accuracy metric is insensitive to correlation effects.**
At ρ=0, effective_n = 7.00 (all votes informative). At ρ=0.9, effective_n = 1.09 (near-useless aggregation). Yet overall accuracy drops only from 0.986 to 0.957 — a 2.9 percentage point drop that appears modest. This is a measurement artifact: binary majority accuracy is dominated by the easy cases and masks degradation on hard/marginal cases.

**Finding 2: FCR is the right metric for adversarial analysis.**
The false consensus rate (fraction of false claims incorrectly ratified) degraded **3.2× from baseline** as ρ went from 0 to 0.9 (1.26% → 4.04%). This is the operationally relevant metric: FCR is where an adversary's attack lands.

**Finding 3: The theoretical effective_n model correctly predicts the direction but overestimates the magnitude of observable accuracy degradation in the clean-signal case.**
Reason: the signal-to-noise ratio in this simulation is strong (+1/-1 signal with unit variance noise). At strong SNR, even correlated noisy voters tend to vote correctly. Correlation effects are amplified when signal is weak (ambiguous claims) or when adversarial contamination exists. These will be measured in SIM-CORR-02 (weak signal) and combined in SIM-RATCHET-01.

**Finding 4: Correlation effects are non-linear and saturating.**
The jump from ρ=0.0 to ρ=0.5 reduces effective_n from 7.00 to 1.75 (75% reduction in information). The jump from ρ=0.5 to ρ=0.9 further reduces it from 1.75 to 1.09 (38% additional reduction). The FCR degradation roughly tracks this: it doubles in the first half, with diminishing additional returns in the second half. The marginal cost of additional correlation decreases as ρ → 1.

**Finding 5: Revised protocol implication.**
The right framing is not "correlation destroys accuracy" but "correlation significantly amplifies the false consensus rate" — the rate at which wrong claims get ratified. A system operating at FCR=1.3% (ρ=0) with 3.2× amplification from correlation reaches FCR=4% (ρ=0.9). If there are, say, 1,000 active claims per epoch, that's 13 false ratifications per epoch at ρ=0 vs. 40 at ρ=0.9. Whether this is acceptable depends on the downstream consequences and the claim reuse weight.

## SIM-CARTEL-01 — Epistemic Minority Capture (Domain Cartel)

**Research question:** At what α(x) does an adversarial cartel achieve reliable control of jury verdicts?

### Results

| |E(x)| | α(x) | α(N) | cartel_success | panel_cartel% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5% (50) | 1% | 0.00% | 0.0275 | 0.0% |
| 5% (50) | 5% | 0.20% | 0.0364 | 4.0% |
| 5% (50) | 10% | 0.50% | 0.0613 | 10.2% |
| 5% (50) | 15% | 0.70% | 0.0767 | 14.4% |
| 5% (50) | 20% | 1.00% | 0.1083 | 19.9% |
| 5% (50) | 30% | 1.50% | 0.1666 | 30.0% |
| 5% (50) | 40% | 2.00% | 0.2375 | 40.2% |
| 10% (100) | 10% | 1.00% | 0.0630 | 10.4% |
| 10% (100) | 20% | 2.00% | 0.1014 | 19.6% |
| 10% (100) | 40% | 4.00% | 0.2409 | 39.8% |
| 20% (200) | 10% | 2.00% | 0.0478 | 10.2% |
| 20% (200) | 20% | 4.00% | 0.1048 | 20.1% |
| 20% (200) | 40% | 8.00% | 0.2968 | 40.1% |

### Prediction verdicts

**SIM-CARTEL-01-P1 REFUTED:** |E(x)|=5%, α(x)=20% achieved cartel_success = 0.1083 (not > 0.50).

**SIM-CARTEL-01-P2 PARTIAL:** CDL-V3 is providing protection, but the mechanism is more subtle than originally described — see Finding 1.

**SIM-CARTEL-01-P3 REFUTED:** |E(x)|=20%, α(x)=40% achieved cartel_success = 0.2968 (not > 0.70). Even at 40% domain control, the cartel succeeds on fewer than 30% of false claims.

### Key findings

**Finding 1: The cartel attack is influence amplification, not direct majority capture.**
The mean cartel fraction on panel ≈ α(x) (e.g., at α(x)=20%, ~1.4 of 7 panelists are adversarial). CDL-V3's 1/3 operator constraint is NOT the primary limit here — the cartel was distributed across operator clusters, so the constraint wasn't binding. The primary limit is arithmetic: 1.4 votes rarely produces a 4-vote majority on its own. The cartel's mechanism is forcing honest voters to generate enough errors (≈16% per vote at these parameters) to provide the remaining votes. This is more fragile than a straightforward majority attack.

**Finding 2: FCR amplification is substantial even without majority control.**
At α(x)=20%, |E(x)|=5%: cartel_success = 10.83%. Baseline FCR from SIM-CORR-01 at ρ=0 was 1.26%. That's an **8.6× amplification** of false consensus rate, achieved with only 20% control of the relevant-expertise pool and 1% control of the total validator population. This is the threat model: not "cartel takes over" but "cartel makes false claims 8× more likely to slip through."

**Finding 3: E(x) size matters less than α(x).**
Across all three |E(x)| sizes, cartel_success at the same α(x) is nearly identical (e.g., at α(x)=40%: 0.2375 vs 0.2409 vs 0.2968 for |E(x)|=5%, 10%, 20%). This is structurally important: **making E(x) larger (more experts) doesn't dilute cartel influence if the cartel scales its resource allocation proportionally.** The only defense is reducing α(x) — i.e., preventing adversarial capture of the expert population, not just expanding it.

**Finding 4: CDL-V3 diversity floor provides limited defense against a multi-cluster cartel.**
Since cartel members were distributed across operator clusters, CDL-V3 did not limit their panel representation. CDL-V3 defends against a cartel that is concentrated in a single operator cluster. A sophisticated cartel that registers validators under multiple operator identities evades CDL-V3 entirely. The Fiedler partition (behavioral graph patterns) is the defense mechanism that could detect this — but only if behavioral patterns diverge from legitimate validators.

**Finding 5: The cartel attack requires sustained resource allocation.**
At α(x)=40%, the cartel controls 40% of the relevant-expertise population across multiple operator clusters. With N=1000 and |E(x)|=5% (50 validators), that's 20 adversarial validators — each consuming stake and reputation. If Werner decay (CDL-V1) is activated, sustained incorrect voting erodes reputation and ECU, making the cartel expensive to maintain. The SIM-RATCHET-01 experiment will model whether the cost is prohibitive.

## Cross-SIM synthesis — What the Phase 1 simulations reveal

### What the protocol handles better than expected

1. **CDL-V3 provides real protection against concentrated cartels.** Against a single-cluster cartel, the 1/3 cap would be binding and effective. The threat is specifically from multi-cluster adversarial coordination.

2. **Majority capture is harder than feared.** At "only" 40% domain expertise control, the cartel succeeds in about 25-30% of false claims — concerning but not catastrophic. Simple majority is never achieved in this parameter range.

3. **The base jury is accurate.** At ρ=0, α(x)=0: FCR=1.26%. This is the baseline quality the protocol can deliver under ideal conditions.

### What remains a genuine open problem

1. **Influence amplification is the real attack surface.** A 20% domain cartel multiplies FCR by 8-9×. In high-stakes domains (medical, legal, financial), even 8-9% false consensus rate on claims that will be widely reused is harmful.

2. **FCR amplification × claim reuse weight is the operationally correct threat model.** A false claim in a high-reuse domain that slips through jury creates a contaminated downstream graph that amplifies through the `raw_reuse_weight` metric. The cartel doesn't need to win every vote — just the high-influence votes.

3. **The ρ×α(x) interaction is unmeasured.** SIM-CORR-01 and SIM-CARTEL-01 were run independently (ρ=0 in cartel, α(x)=0 in correlation). When both are nonzero simultaneously — a cartel of correlated validators in a domain — the effects likely compound. This is the SIM-RATCHET-01 scenario.

4. **Weak-signal claims are the real vulnerability.** Both SIMs used a strong +1/-1 signal. Real expert disagreement on genuinely ambiguous claims will have a much weaker signal, making correlation effects and cartel influence dramatically more impactful. SIM-CORR-02 should vary signal strength.

### Recommended next experiments

1. **SIM-RATCHET-01** — Combined ρ and α(x) nonzero; vary (ρ, α(x)) jointly; measure FCR surface
2. **SIM-CORR-02** — Vary signal strength (strong → ambiguous → marginal); measure where correlation starts dominating
3. **SIM-LAUNDER-01** — Graph reuse weight model; measure how a false claim propagates through downstream citations

---

## Protocol implications for post-RC roadmap

1. **FCR amplification × claim-influence weighting** should be the design target for the priority-weighting mechanism (risk × influence × uncertainty). Phase 1 simulations confirm this is the correct formulation.

2. **CDL-V3 multi-cluster evasion** is the cartel's primary vulnerability exploit and should be named explicitly in the security threat model. The Fiedler behavioral partition is the primary detection mechanism.

3. **Weak-signal claims** need differentiated jury composition and/or sequential deliberation. Strong-signal claims (ρ-independent accuracy 96%+) don't need the same protection as ambiguous technical claims.

4. **Werner decay activation** is the economic countermeasure. SIM-RATCHET-01 should be designed to test whether the Werner phase boundary (δ > R) holds under the cartel parameters observed here.
