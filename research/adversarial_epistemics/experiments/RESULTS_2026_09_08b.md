## Methodological correction (adopted from Astra 6.0)

All FCR values in this program are **model results**, not empirical protocol measurements.
The voter model assigns `signal_strength` as a latent parameter; "weak signal → high FCR"
is a consequence of the assumed voter error distribution, not an empirical claim about ILC.
The correct framing is: *"Under this voter-error distribution, a 0.2-signal claim produces
16% FCR."* Language in prior results documents occasionally slipped toward empirical claims;
that was an error. This document uses model-result framing throughout.

Additionally: the `independent_evidence_weight` and `epistemic_weight` functions tested in
SIM-LAUNDER-01 are **research proposals**, not current ILC implementation. The live protocol
uses `raw_reuse_weight` without provenance depth discounting.

## SIM-SIGNAL-01 — Can the Protocol Detect That It Doesn't Know?

**Research question:** Can observable probe-panel disagreement discriminate hard claims from
easy ones, without seeing the latent `signal_strength`?

### Disagreement by signal level

| signal_strength | mean_disagreement (probe=5) | mean_disagreement (probe=15) | probe_accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 (strong)   | 0.136 | 0.157 | ~98% |
| 0.7            | 0.183 | 0.237 | ~90% |
| 0.5 (medium)   | 0.211 | 0.298 | ~86% |
| 0.3            | 0.243 | 0.345 | ~73% |
| 0.2 (weak)     | 0.251 | 0.363 | ~67% |
| 0.1 (marginal) | 0.244 | 0.374 | ~58% |

### AUC results (probe=5, using vote disagreement as classifier)

| Comparison | AUC | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| strong(1.0) vs weak(0.2)     | 0.615 | Poor |
| strong(1.0) vs marginal(0.1) | 0.608 | Poor |
| strong(1.0) vs medium(0.5)   | 0.575 | Near-chance |
| medium(0.5) vs weak(0.2)     | 0.540 | Near-chance |
| weak(0.2) vs marginal(0.1)   | 0.493 | Chance |

AUC with probe=15 for strong vs weak: **0.889** — a major improvement.

### Prediction verdicts

**P1 REFUTED:** AUC(strong vs weak) = 0.615 at probe=5 (threshold was >0.75). ✗
**P2 CONFIRMED:** AUC(medium vs weak) = 0.540 < 0.65 — adjacent classes are nearly indistinguishable. ✓
**P3 REFUTED:** At optimal threshold (0.25), sensitivity=0.502, false_escalation=0.271. Sensitivity well below 80%. ✗
**P4 REFUTED:** Probe size=15 raises AUC from 0.615 to 0.889 — a 0.274 improvement, far above the <0.05pp threshold. ✗

### Key findings

**Finding 1: A 5-member probe panel cannot reliably detect difficulty. A 15-member probe can — for extreme cases.**
The mean disagreement gradient is clear (strong=0.136, weak=0.251) but the distributions overlap
substantially. At probe=5, AUC=0.615 — better than chance but not operationally useful for routing.
At probe=15, AUC=0.889 for strong vs weak — usable. The protocol *could* implement a preliminary
15-member diversity probe before routing, but this doubles evaluation cost for every claim.

**Finding 2: Adjacent difficulty levels are nearly indistinguishable (AUC~0.54).**
The most practically important distinction — medium vs weak — has AUC=0.540 at probe=5.
Marginal vs weak: AUC=0.493 (coin flip). The protocol cannot distinguish "this claim is
ambiguous but answerable" from "this claim is genuinely indeterminate" using vote patterns alone.

**Finding 3: The reviewer's circularity concern is confirmed.**
P3 is refuted because at optimal threshold, sensitivity=0.502 and false_escalation=0.271.
For every 2 hard claims correctly escalated, 1 easy claim is incorrectly escalated.
This is the circularity: the same jury that is confused about a hard claim generates
the ambiguity signal used to route it. The routing mechanism has signal, but not enough
to operate safely without adversarial probe manipulation (see SIM-CAMOUFLAGE-01).

**Finding 4: Claim routing is implementable with a 15-member probe, but with caveats.**
AUC=0.889 for strong vs weak at probe=15 is genuinely useful. At balanced threshold,
a 15-member probe could catch ~80% of truly weak-signal claims while escalating ~20%
of strong-signal claims unnecessarily. Whether that cost is acceptable is a design decision,
not a simulation finding. The adversarial probe manipulation concern remains unaddressed.

**Finding 5: Weak and marginal signal are computationally indistinguishable.**
AUC(weak vs marginal) = 0.493. The protocol cannot distinguish a claim that is hard-but-resolvable
from a claim that is genuinely beyond current epistemic reach. Both produce ~25% disagreement
on a 5-member probe. This is the deeper problem: you cannot route "escalate for bigger panel"
vs "mark as PERSISTENTLY_CONTESTED" without additional information.

## SIM-PANEL-01 — Panel Size vs FCR Reduction (2.4M trials)

**Research question:** How much does larger jury size buy? Does it plateau under correlation?

### FCR by panel size — rho=0, alpha=0 (baseline, no adversary or correlation)

| Signal | n=7 | n=15 | n=31 | n=63 | n=127 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| strong   | 1.4% | 0.2% | 0.0% | 0.0% | **0.0%** |
| medium   | 9.5% | 4.2% | 2.7% | 0.4% | **0.0%** |
| weak     | 19.5% | 20.0% | 17.2% | 11.6% | **5.6%** |
| marginal | 25.9% | 27.8% | 28.0% | 25.4% | **20.6%** |

### FCR by panel size — rho=0.9, alpha=0 (high correlation, no adversary)

| Signal | n=7 | n=15 | n=31 | n=63 | n=127 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| strong   | 2.7% | 1.7% | 0.8% | 0.4% | 0.2% |
| medium   | 11.9% | 11.6% | 9.8% | 7.5% | **9.1%** |
| weak     | 23.0% | 26.4% | 27.0% | 28.9% | **27.1%** |
| marginal | 27.0% | 33.4% | 36.3% | 36.4% | **36.3%** |

### Prediction verdicts

**P1 CONFIRMED:** At medium signal, rho=0, panel doublings:
  - n=7→15: FCR 9.5%→4.2% (ratio=0.44) — better than halving ✓
  - n=15→31: FCR 4.2%→2.7% (ratio=0.65) — approximately half ✓
  - n=31→63: FCR 2.7%→0.4% (ratio=0.14) — super-linear improvement ✓

**P2 CONFIRMED** (but with unexpected twist): rho=0.9, panel 7→127 FCR change:
  - strong: Δ=2.5pp ✓
  - medium: Δ=2.9pp ✓
  - weak: **Δ=−4.1pp** — FCR *increases* with larger panels at high correlation ✓ (unexpected)
  - marginal: **Δ=−9.4pp** — FCR increases strongly at high correlation ✓ (unexpected)

**P3 CONFIRMED:** At rho=0, medium signal, alpha=0%: n=127 achieves FCR≈0.0% ✓

**P4 CONFIRMED** (voter-model result): At weak signal, rho=0: n=127 achieves FCR=5.6%, not below 0.
  At marginal: n=127 achieves FCR=20.6%. These floor values are consequences of the voter model. ✓

### Key findings — the most important result in the entire simulation program

**Finding 1: Under high correlation, larger panels make FCR WORSE for weak/marginal signals.**

This is the central result. At rho=0.9, weak signal:
- n=7: FCR=23.0%
- n=15: FCR=26.4%
- n=31: FCR=27.0%
- n=63: FCR=28.9%
- n=127: FCR=27.1%

Mechanism: When validators are highly correlated, they share a group-level bias.
A larger correlated panel samples more of the same shared error, not more independent
evidence. As panel size grows, the majority vote converges to the direction of the
shared bias — which is as likely to be wrong as right on a weak-signal claim.
At n=127 with rho=0.9, you have effectively 1.1 independent votes; adding 120 more
correlated votes just amplifies the single shared error direction.

**This directly contradicts the reviewer's suggestion that panel size might solve the
medium-signal problem.** Under the critical parameter combination (high correlation +
weak signal), larger panels are actively harmful.

**Finding 2: Panel size is highly effective under independence (rho=0).**
At medium signal, rho=0: n=127 achieves near-zero FCR. The protocol can be made
very safe on medium-signal claims by using large panels IF validators are uncorrelated.
The independence assumption is critical — not a minor detail.

**Finding 3: The safe regime is narrower than previously stated.**
The protocol's epistemic guarantees (in this model) hold only for:
  - Strong signal: any panel size, any rho
  - Medium signal + rho≤0.4 + alpha≤20%: FCR < 10% at n≥31
  - Medium signal + rho≥0.5: FCR remains 9-12% regardless of panel size

**Finding 4: Panel size and correlation interact non-trivially.**
The crossing point: for medium signal, rho=0.9, FCR is non-monotone (9.5% at n=7,
then 11.6% at n=63, then back to 9.1% at n=127). This suggests a complex interaction
between the panel CDL-V3 cluster constraint and correlation structure at large panel
sizes — worth investigating analytically.

**Finding 5: Weak-signal claims cannot be fixed by panel size alone.**
At weak signal, rho=0.5, n=127 still produces ~15-17% FCR. The model result is clear:
for intrinsically ambiguous claims, no panel size substitutes for either (a) better
signal (more evidence) or (b) lower validator correlation.

## SIM-LAUNDER-01 — Graph Propagation and Epistemic Laundering

**Research question:** Does the graph stage amplify jury FCR into entrenched false claims?
Can graph weight functions distinguish independent corroboration from propagation?

### Population results (200 runs × 60 epochs)

| Population | raw_reuse_weight | independent_evidence | epistemic_weight | refuted_fraction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — genuine corroboration (10 indep sources, True) | 4.67 | **10.00** | **9.09** | 0.265 |
| B — pure propagation (1 source, True)              | **4.87** | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.240 |
| B — pure propagation (1 source, False)             | 4.73 | 1.00 | 1.00 | **0.870** |
| C — cartel (5 coords. false sources)               | 4.58 | 1.00 | 0.91 | 0.435 |
| D — mixed (3 indep. + propagation, True)           | 4.49 | 3.00 | 2.73 | 0.250 |

### Temporal lock-in — B_false_propagation

| Epoch | P(entrenched, RRW≥5, not refuted) |
|---|---|
| 10 | 0.00% |
| 20 | 0.00% |
| 30 | 4.50% |
| 45 | 5.50% |
| 60 | 7.00% |

### Ranking

- By raw_reuse_weight: B_true > A > C > D ← **WRONG epistemic order**
- By independent_evidence: **A > D > B_true > C** ← **CORRECT epistemic order**

### Prediction verdicts

**P1 CONFIRMED:** A_RRW=4.67 ≈ B_true_RRW=4.87 (diff=0.20, 4%). Pure propagation is indistinguishable
from genuine corroboration by raw_reuse_weight alone. ✓

**P2 REFUTED:** A_IEW=10.00, B_IEW=1.00, **C_IEW=1.00** — B and C are tied. The claim was that
independent_evidence ranks A >> B >> C. It correctly ranks A >> {B,C} but B=C (both have
independent_origin=1 since B has 1 source, C's cartel all share origin=0). ✗ (partial — A is
correctly separated, but B and C are not differentiated by IEW alone.)

**P3 REFUTED:** P(entrenched by epoch 30) = 4.5% (threshold was >30%). The refutation mechanism
is effective — B_false is refuted 87% of the time by epoch 60. ✗

**P4 implicitly confirmed:** A_genuine_corroboration refuted_fraction=0.265, which is the true-claim
baseline false-positive refutation rate. Genuine corroboration is not epistemically entrenched in the
wrong direction.

**P5 CONFIRMED:** C_RRW=4.58 ≈ D_RRW=4.49 (indistinguishable by RRW). C_IEW=1.00 vs D_IEW=3.00
(factor of 3× difference — cartel correctly scored lower). ✓

### Key findings

**Finding 1: The refutation mechanism is the load-bearing defense against epistemic laundering.**
B_false_propagation is refuted 87% of the time by epoch 60. The model's refutation rate
(P_REFUTE_FALSE=3.5% per epoch) is sufficient to prevent lock-in under these parameters.
However: the cartel condition (70% protection factor) drops B_false to 43.5% refuted — a 2×
reduction in the refutation mechanism's effectiveness. Whether real-world refutation rates
match these model parameters is unknown; this is the critical empirical question.

**Finding 2: raw_reuse_weight actively ranks the populations in the WRONG epistemic order.**
B_true (pure propagation, 1 source) ranks ABOVE A (genuine corroboration, 10 sources) by RRW.
This is a genuine architectural finding: the current weight metric rewards reuse volume, not
independent evidence count. A single claim that gets heavily cited looks epistemically stronger
than a genuinely well-corroborated claim with fewer citations.

**Finding 3: independent_evidence_weight correctly separates A from {B,C,D}.**
A_IEW=10.00 (10 independent sources), D_IEW=3.00 (3 sources), B_IEW=C_IEW=1.00.
The metric works for genuine vs. propagated distinction. It fails to distinguish B (honest
propagation) from C (coordinated cartel) because both present as single-origin. Differentiating
C from B requires behavioral analysis (Fiedler partition) or explicit coordination detection —
not derivable from the citation graph topology alone.

**Finding 4: The cartel attack's primary effect is suppressing refutation, not building evidence.**
C_cartel: refuted_fraction=0.435 vs B_false: 0.870. The cartel's 70% protection factor halves
the refutation rate. The cartel doesn't need to create compelling evidence — it just needs to
prevent the honest refutation mechanism from reaching the claim. This is consistent with the
SECURITY.md "epistemic camouflage" attack pattern: strategic non-challenge of cartel claims.

**Finding 5: True epistemic lock-in (P3) is rare under current model parameters.**
Only 7% of B_false cases become entrenched by epoch 60. This is partly because the refutation
rate is high (3.5%/epoch for false claims without protection), and the entrenched threshold
(RRW≥5) requires substantial citation accumulation in a short window. A lower refutation rate
(representing a less active honest community) or a higher citation rate would produce more
lock-in. The model parameters are the critical unknown.

## Cross-SIM synthesis — 7 SIMs, 4M+ simulated claims

### The complete picture

After seven simulations, the threat model has substantially converged. The reviewer's
framework has been largely confirmed at the structural level, with important quantitative
corrections.

**The protocol is safe for:**
- Strong-signal claims at any rho, any alpha≤20%, any panel size
- Medium-signal claims at rho≤0.4, alpha≤10%, panel size≥31 (under independence)

**The protocol is unsafe (in this model) for:**
- Weak/marginal-signal claims regardless of panel size or validator diversity
- Medium-signal claims under high correlation (rho≥0.5) — FCR remains 9-18%
- Any claim where refutation suppression is effective (cartel protection ~2× FCR)

**The three-tier epistemic state architecture (reviewer's proposal) is directly supported:**
SIM-SIGNAL-01 confirms that weak and marginal signal are computationally indistinguishable
from probe-panel vote patterns alone. The correct protocol response is not to force a binary
verdict but to declare PERSISTENTLY_CONTESTED. SIM-PANEL-01 confirms that larger panels
cannot resolve this. SIM-LAUNDER-01 confirms that refutation is the load-bearing defense.

**The load-bearing architectural gap identified:**
The current `raw_reuse_weight` metric ranks pure propagation (1 source, many citations) ABOVE
genuine corroboration (10 sources, fewer citations). An `independent_evidence_weight` metric
would correct this. This is the most actionable architectural finding from the simulation
program — it requires a CDL to formalize and a runtime change to implement.
